Taj Mahal

The marble mausoleum Shah Jahan built for Mumtaz Mahal

LocationAgra & Fatehpur Sikri
Built1632–1653
UNESCOYes (1983)
Allow2–3 hours
OpenClosed on Fridays
Best lightSunrise or late afternoon

Overview

The Taj Mahal was commissioned by the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan in memory of his wife Mumtaz Mahal. Built of white marble on the bank of the Yamuna, it is widely regarded as the finest example of Mughal architecture.

The mausoleum stands on a raised platform with four minarets, at the end of a formal charbagh garden. Its marble changes colour through the day: pale pink at dawn, white at noon and golden in late light.

What to look for

  • The pietra dura inlay of semi-precious stones set into the marble
  • The calligraphy framing the main arch, scaled so the letters look even from the ground
  • The view back along the garden from the terrace
  • Mehtab Bagh across the river for the classic view without the crowds

Visiting tips

  • The Taj Mahal is closed on Fridays.
  • Choose the earliest entry: the first hour is calmest and the light is softest.
  • Security is strict. Bring only a small bag and leave tripods, food and large electronics behind.
  • Dress modestly. Shoe covers or bare feet are needed on the marble platform.
  • Ticket prices and rules change, so check them before you travel.
