Chausath Yogini Temple, Mitawali

A circular temple of 64 shrines open to the sky

Overview

The Chausath Yogini Temple at Mitawali, near Morena in Madhya Pradesh, is a circular temple of sandstone on a small hill. A ring of 64 small shrines for the yoginis, female divinities of a Tantric tradition, surrounds an open courtyard with a central shrine to Shiva. The temple has no roof over the courtyard, which is open to the sky.

It is usually dated to the eleventh century, under the Kachchhapaghata dynasty, although an inscription dated 1323 CE names the king Devapala. A popular belief links its round plan with the design of the Indian Parliament building, but this is not proven.

What to look for

  • The circular ring of 64 chambers
  • The central Shiva shrine in the open courtyard
  • The hilltop views over the countryside

Visiting tips

  • It is a simple, quiet site: allow about 45 minutes.
  • It lies off the main road between Agra and Gwalior, so plan the detour with your guide.
  • Wear shoes with grip on the stone steps up the hill.
