Overview

The Viceregal Lodge in Shimla was built between 1884 and 1888 as the summer residence of the British Viceroy of India. It was designed by the architect Henry Irwin in a grey limestone Jacobethan style, with a tower and wooden interiors.

Important meetings were held here, including the Simla Conference of 1945. Since 1965 it has housed the Indian Institute of Advanced Study, and part of the building and grounds are open to visitors.

What to look for

  • The grey limestone facade and tower
  • The wood-panelled interiors and the library
  • The room where the Simla Conference was held
  • The gardens and the Himalayan views

Visiting tips

  • Entry is by ticket, with access to parts of the building; check timings.
  • Photography rules apply inside; check at the gate.
  • Allow about 90 minutes.
