Chandni Chowk

Old Delhi's great market street

LocationDelhi
BuiltLaid out in the 1650s
UNESCONo
Allow1.5–2 hours
OpenMany shops close on Sundays
Best lightMorning

Overview

Chandni Chowk is the main market street of Old Delhi, laid out in the 1650s as part of Shah Jahan's new city, Shahjahanabad. It runs from the Red Fort toward the Fatehpuri Masjid and is traditionally said to have been planned by his daughter Jahanara Begum.

Today its crowded lanes hold spice markets, jewellery shops, sweet shops and street food, along with a Jain temple, a Sikh gurdwara, a church and a mosque on or near the main road. A rickshaw ride through the lanes is the classic way to see it.

What to look for

  • The spice market at Khari Baoli nearby
  • The Paranthe Wali Gali for fried flatbreads
  • The Gurdwara Sis Ganj Sahib and the Jain temple
  • The Red Fort at one end of the street

Visiting tips

  • Go in the morning, when it is calmer; it is very crowded later.
  • A cycle-rickshaw ride with your guide is the best way through the lanes.
  • Keep valuables secure and carry small notes.
