Humayun's Tomb

The garden-tomb that inspired the Taj Mahal

LocationDelhi
BuiltAbout 1565–1572
UNESCOYes (1993)
AllowAbout 1.5 hours
OpenUsually open daily (check locally)
Best lightLate afternoon

Overview

Humayun's Tomb is the first monumental garden-tomb in India and a forerunner of the Taj Mahal. It was commissioned by Humayun's widow, Hamida Banu Begum, and designed by the Persian architect Mirak Mirza Ghiyas.

Red sandstone and white marble, a double dome and a charbagh garden divided by water channels make it one of Delhi's most harmonious monuments. It has been carefully restored in recent years.

What to look for

  • The garden layout and its water channels
  • The double dome and the contrast of red sandstone with white marble
  • The Isa Khan tomb enclosure near the entrance
  • The Barber's Tomb, a small domed tomb in the grounds

Visiting tips

  • Allow about 90 minutes.
  • The grounds are quiet and shaded in late afternoon.
  • Pair it with Qutub Minar for a South Delhi half-day.
  • Wear comfortable shoes for the paths and platforms.
