Gurudwara Bangla Sahib

A Sikh shrine in the heart of New Delhi

LocationDelhi
BuiltPresent building late 18th century
UNESCONo
Allow1–1.5 hours
OpenOpen daily, all day (check locally)

Overview

Gurudwara Bangla Sahib in Connaught Place is one of Delhi's most important Sikh shrines. It is associated with the eighth Sikh Guru, Guru Har Krishan, who stayed here in 1664 during a smallpox epidemic, and the water of its sarovar, the sacred pool, is revered for its healing power.

The golden-domed gurdwara is open to everyone. Its langar, the community kitchen, serves free vegetarian meals to thousands of visitors every day.

What to look for

  • The golden dome reflected in the sarovar
  • Kirtan, the devotional singing, in the main hall
  • The langar kitchen where volunteers cook and serve
  • The marble courtyard around the pool

Visiting tips

  • Cover your head, remove your shoes and wash your feet at the entrance; head scarves are available.
  • You are welcome to eat in the langar: sit with everyone and take only what you will finish.
  • Tobacco and alcohol are not allowed in the complex.
