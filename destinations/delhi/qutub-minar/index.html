Qutub Minar

A towering Delhi Sultanate victory tower

LocationDelhi
BuiltFrom about 1200 CE
UNESCOYes (1993)
Allow1–1.5 hours
OpenUsually open daily (check locally)
Best lightMorning

Overview

The Qutub Minar is a victory tower of red sandstone and marble, about 73 metres high, and one of the finest early Indo-Islamic monuments in Delhi. It was begun around 1200 CE by Qutb ud-Din Aibak and completed by later Delhi Sultans.

It stands in a complex that includes the Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque, the ornate Alai Darwaza and the Iron Pillar, a Gupta-era pillar that has resisted rust for more than 1,500 years.

What to look for

  • Bands of Quranic inscription and carved balconies on the minaret
  • The Iron Pillar in the mosque courtyard
  • The carved arches of the Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque, built with reused temple pillars
  • Alai Darwaza, the Khalji gateway with delicate inlaid marble

Visiting tips

  • Visitors can no longer climb the minaret.
  • Go in the morning to avoid heat and groups.
  • Combine it with Humayun's Tomb in a South Delhi half-day.
  • The complex is large, so wear comfortable shoes.
